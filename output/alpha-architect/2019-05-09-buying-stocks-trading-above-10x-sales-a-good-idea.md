# Buying Stocks Trading Above 10x Sales–A Good Idea?

> Early last week, Meb Faber included me on a conversation on buying stocks trading at 10x their company’s revenue (sales). Is this a good idea […]

Early last week, Meb Faber included me on a conversation on buying stocks trading at 10x their company’s revenue (sales).

Is this a good idea and how did it do in the past?

![](https://alphaarchitect.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/buying-stocks-trading-above-10x-sales-1-1-600x614.png)

Given that most “known” factors have underperformed over the past 10 years, I was interested in seeing if a somewhat “crazy” strategy(1)–buying stocks trading above 10x sales–worked over the past 10 years.

Of course–it worked!

However, over the full sample period, this strategy underperformed the universe.

Needless to say, the results are interesting, especially after examining the current stocks that are trading above 10x revenue.

Below I outline the study, give the results, and look at a few names currently in the “above 10x” category.

## Overview and Universe Results

To test how well stocks trading above or below 10x revenue (sales) performed, I formed monthly portfolios examining companies trading above and below 10x revenue (sales). The universe, formed monthly, is the 1,500 largest U.S. stocks excluding REITs.(2) When forming the portfolios, the fundamental data is lagged to minimize look-ahead bias.(3)(4) All data is from FactSet.

The full-time period is from 1/1/1990-3/31/2019.

Below shows the results of the Universe (value-weighted) compared to the Russell 1000 Index (R1K). All results are gross of any transaction costs and fees and reflect total returns, which include dividends.

![](https://alphaarchitect.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/buying-stocks-trading-above-10x-sales-2-1-600x291.png)

The results above show that the universe is similar to the Russell 1000, with a 99.77% correlation.(5) In addition, the two portfolios have similar CAGRs, Sharpe Ratios, and Drawdowns.

So now that we have confidence in our universe, let’s examine the portfolios trading above and below 10x revenue.

The results above highlight a few items:

1. Over the entire time period, buying stocks trading above 10x Revenue was a bad idea!
2. Over the past 10 years, buying stocks trading above 10x revenue beat the market! One can see this on the “10-year Annualized Return” row of the table.(6)
3. Buying stocks above 10x revenue resulted in an 81.37% maximum drawdown!

## Which Stocks Trade at 10x Sales?

First, how many firms are actually trading at 10x sales? Below I show the time-series of the two portfolios:

![](https://alphaarchitect.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/buying-stocks-trading-above-10x-sales-6-800x583.png)

Source: FactSet

As one can see, most firms are trading below 10x sales. However, there are a decent number of firms above 10x sales. The average number of firms over the entire time period was 70 firms, with the median being 59 firms.(8)

So who is currently trading above 10x revenue? Below I show the 14 largest firms as of 3/29/2019:

## Conclusion

So what should we make of these results? First, over the past 10 years, buying stocks trading above a relatively high level of sales (10x) was actually a decent strategy as it would have beat the market (before transaction costs and taxes!). However, over a longer time period, this was a bad bet.

It should be highlighted that this was a monthly-rebalanced strategy, which makes the portfolio trading above 10x sales a “momentum-ish” portfolio. Over the 1/1/1990-12/31/2018, the portfolio trading above 10x sales did have a positive and significant loading on the momentum factor.

So while we say “[Never buy expensive stocks, period](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/07/01/value-investing-never-buy-expensive-stocks-period/)“, perhaps this study highlights that there may be times (such as the last 10 years!) when this strategy works.
