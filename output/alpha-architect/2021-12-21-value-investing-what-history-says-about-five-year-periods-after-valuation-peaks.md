# Value investing: What history says about five-year periods after valuation peaks

> Value stocks are historically cheap compared to the past. Given this fact, a natural question is the following, “After the last two times Value had a “peak” of the factor being cheap, how did it do the subsequent five years?”

No matter how you slice it, [value stocks](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/10/07/the-quantitative-value-investing-philosophy/) are historically cheap compared to the past. Here’s what history says about the five year periods after valuation peaks.

## Value investing and prior valuation peaks

There have been numerous articles on this topic, such as Ryan’s post about the [value factor](https://alphaarchitect.com/2021/11/23/the-value-of-value-cheaper-now-than-a-year-ago/), Larry Swedroe’s post about the [cheapness of value stocks,](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2021/11/22/how-cheap-are-value-stocks) and more recently, Cliff Asness’ post about [value spreads](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives/Thats-it-Thats-the-Blog).

Cliff’s post is one picture, shown below.(1)

![](https://alphaarchitect.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/value.five_.year_.stretch.1.png)

Source: https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives/Thats-it-Thats-the-Blog

The image above shows the relative “cheapness” of Value using a value composite measure across multiple countries.(2)

What one notices is that there are three peaks around the following dates:

1. Internet Bubble–12/31/1999.
2. Global Financial Crisis–12/31/2008.
3. Today–near the end of December 2021.

On our website, we also have a tool, our [Visual Value Factors](https://alphaarchitect.com/visualfactors/) module, that allows individuals to see how cheap/expensive Value portfolios are on a variety of measures.(3)

In summary, in both the U.S. market, as well as Developed International markets(4), Value is cheap relative to the past, with the same three peaks as AQR.

We find similar results using B/M and E/P measures.

Given this fact, a natural question I was asked last week during a meeting was the following, “After the last two times Value had a “peak” of the factor being cheap, how did value investing do the subsequent five years?”

A good question.

To answer that, we can examine how the top decile of Value stocks did in both the U.S. and international markets the five-year stretch after 12/31/1999 and 12/31/2008.

So let’s move onto the next valuation peak–12/31/2008.

## Conclusions on Value Investing and valuation peaks

With the caveat that no one can predict the future, this article outlines how Value Investing did after the prior two valuation peaks.

As shown above, in the past two instances of a valuation peak in value portfolios, the next five-year stretch was a good time to be a Value investor. However, as we all know, history is not a perfect indicator of the future and it is difficult to time the valuation peak and it can go on for years at a time. For example, value has been relatively cheap the last 2+ years, only to lose to the market and growth investing.
