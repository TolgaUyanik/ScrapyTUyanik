# Inspiring Market Efficiency Results…NOT!

> Yes, it is true, lower risk securities have historically outperformed higher risk securities. We’ve seen this in a variety of academic research pieces, but sometimes […]

Yes, it is true, lower risk securities have historically outperformed higher risk securities. We’ve seen this in a variety of academic research pieces, but sometimes you gotta grind the data on your own to really understand and get comfortable with the results. A [recent paper](https://alphaarchitect.com//2014/06/09/betting-beta-demand-lottery/#.U_UG1vm9mls) and [this paper](https://alphaarchitect.com//2013/08/14/intelligent-thoughts-on-low-vol-investing/#.U_UHNfm9mls) and some of our own internal research has suggested that the “low vol” anomaly [might be bunk](https://alphaarchitect.com//2014/03/10/low-volatility-anomaly-lacks-robustness/#.U_UHG_m9mls). We are on a data adventure to get to the bottom of this anomaly…

In the meantime, gleam over the numbers.

There is definitely a counter-intuitive relationship between risk and return. **Higher risk, lower return; lower risk, higher return.**

The difference between IVOL and BETA are trivial and highly correlated (90%+).

**But are these spreads robust? Are they other anomalies in drag? We are going to find out…**
